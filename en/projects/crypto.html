Personal exploration of cryptography in Python

Introduction

I started implementing cryptographic algorithms to understand their mathematical logic and internal mechanics. The objective was never to replace established libraries, but to learn by rebuilding simplified versions and observing their limitations.

1. Monoalphabetic substitution

Each letter is replaced according to a fixed alphabet. A simple first step that makes the weakness of deterministic substitution visible.

…

2. Vigenère cipher

A repeated key determines the shift applied to each letter and introduces the idea of polyalphabetic substitution.

…

3. Transposition cipher

Characters are preserved but reordered, illustrating the role of permutations.

…

4. One-Time Pad (OTP)

Character-by-character XOR with an equally long key. The experiment helps visualize the plaintext, key and ciphertext representations and the requirement that a true OTP key be random, secret and never reused.

5. AES (ECB / CBC)

A simplified learning implementation used to study SubBytes, ShiftRows and MixColumns, with the goal of understanding diffusion and confusion.

…

6. RSA

A learning implementation used to explore modular arithmetic, asymmetric encryption and the performance trade-offs of public-key operations.

…

Conclusion

This collection documents my progression from classical substitution and transposition to block and public-key cryptography. Its purpose is educational: understanding a primitive also means understanding where a homemade implementation stops being secure.
