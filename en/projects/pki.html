PQC-OnionPKI

Conception et déploiement d’une infrastructure de confiance post-quantique sur Raspberry Pi, durcie et exposée via un service Tor onion. Le projet explore une migration réaliste vers la PQC en combinant une PKI compatible avec l’écosystème actuel et une ML-DSA-65 signing appliquée automatiquement aux e-mails au niveau du relais SMTP.

ML-DSA-65OpenSSL-OQS / liboqsX.509PostfixTor Onion ServiceRaspberry Pi 4PythonLinux hardening

Objective

The goal was to go beyond a simple demonstration of a post-quantum algorithm and test PQC as a real infrastructure service: key generation and protection, trust chain, signing, controlled network exposure and integration into an existing application.

E-mail was deliberately chosen as a constrained use case in order to observe the actual limitations of clients, MTAs and legacy formats.

Platform

  • Raspberry Pi 4 dedicated to the PKI and signing service.
  • Debian with SSH hardening, UFW/nftables and Fail2ban.
  • Administrative access through a Tor onion service, without direct public-IP exposure.
  • Private keys isolated from application directories with restrictive permissions.

Trust architecture

100% post-quantum PKI — experimental

A complete Root CA → Mail CA → application-certificate chain was generated with post-quantum signatures through OpenSSL-OQS/liboqs. Although cryptographically functional, it highlights a current limitation: major operating systems and mail clients do not yet natively support post-quantum X.509 certificates.

Hybrid PKI — operational

La couche de compatibilité reste classique pour les certificats et l’authentification SMTP/TLS, tandis que l’intégrité et l’authenticité du message sont protégées indépendamment par une ML-DSA-65 signing. Cette séparation permet de conserver les clients existants sans leur imposer de support PQC natif.

E-mail signing chain

1. Postfixoutgoing message reception
2. Canonicalizationnormalized headers + text/plain body
3. FingerprintSHA-256 via OpenSSL
4. PQC signatureML-DSA-65
5. SMTP reinjectionproof attached, then relayed

Le script pqc_sign_mail.py est utilisé comme content_filter Postfix et s’exécute sous un utilisateur non privilégié. La représentation du message est déterministe afin de pouvoir reproduire la verification côté serveur.

Performance on Raspberry Pi

≈ 19 mskey generation
≈ 20 msML-DSA-65 signing
≈ 19 msverification

These measurements show that post-quantum signing remains compatible with synchronous e-mail processing even on inexpensive ARM hardware.

What the project highlighted

  • PQC X.509 certificates remain incompatible with a significant part of the current client ecosystem.
  • Custom X-PQC-* headers correctly generated server-side can be removed or hidden by Gmail, Apple Mail and Outlook.
  • The proof must therefore be designed at both system and application level, not only at the cryptographic primitive level.
  • Moving from a proof carried in headers to a visible proof in the content illustrates this adaptation to real-world constraints.

Prototype traces

Current intentional limits

The prototype focuses on text/plain messages and deliberately exposes current client limitations. The goal is precisely to document the gap between a cryptographically valid proof and its integration into a legacy ecosystem.
