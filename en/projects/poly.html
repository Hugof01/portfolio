FRENDE

Polymorphic engines & PRNG

Experimental study of a polymorphic engine for pseudo-random generation

Introduction

This project started from a simple question: could the behavior of a polymorphic engine — a system that continuously changes form while preserving an internal logic — be represented visually and used as the basis of a pseudo-random generator? The objective was not to create a conventional PRNG, but to explore a more geometric and organic view of “entropy”.

1. Polymorphic engine model

The first representation is a dynamic network. Each vertex represents a point in the system and each edge an interaction. The graph evolves according to input parameters.

2. Deformable sphere

The network is then projected onto a sphere. Vertices become points on the surface and can move slightly toward or away from the center, introducing a form of geometric variability. These movements influence the generated characters.

3. Rotation

A continuous rotation adds a temporal dimension. Each vertex follows a trajectory through space, and the changing state contributes to character generation.

4. Combining both sources

Deformation and rotation are combined. The engine uses vertex position, speed, direction and distance from the center to determine the next textual value.

5. Experimental text generation

The final experiment maps the instantaneous state of the engine to pseudo-random text. The output is not claimed to be cryptographically random: it emerges from the internal dynamics of the geometric system.

Conclusion & perspective

This project helped me explore pseudo-random generation visually and intuitively. At this stage I consider the engine more of an artistic and experimental model than a mathematical cryptographic primitive. Open questions include mapping spatial changes to characters, tuning rotation/deformation to balance distributions and studying whether controlled sequences can be produced for a defined objective.
